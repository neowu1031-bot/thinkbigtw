Giving a Custom Book to a Friend's Child: How Do You Get the Photos, and Who Do You Ask First?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-08   ·  中文

The short answer

You can give a custom picture book as a gift for a friend's child—but you cannot collect the photos yourself. Under Taiwan's Civil Code Article 1086, parents are the legal representatives of their minor children. Under Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, the child's photos are personal data belonging to the child, controlled by the parent. This means the parent of the friend's child must be the one who submits the photos, not you. You can initiate and pay for the gift; the parent must do the photo steps.

1. Legal framework

Civil Code Article 1086: Parents are the legal guardians and representatives of their minor children. This means only a parent (or legal guardian) can make decisions about their child's personal data.

Personal Data Protection Act, Article 7: Consent to personal data collection must be specific, informed, and given voluntarily by the data subject or their legal representative. For a child's photo, the legal representative is the parent. PDPA Article 8 requires that the data subject (through their representative) be informed of the purpose, recipients, and duration before consenting.

The implication for custom book gifts: if you are the gift giver but not the child's parent, you do not have legal authority to submit the child's photos on your own initiative.

2. Why you cannot use photos from social media

Taking a photo from a friend’s public social media post and submitting it to a custom book service does three things wrong at once:

  • It uses the photo for a purpose the person never consented to when they shared it online.
  • It submits the child's photo to a third-party processor (AI, cloud storage) without the parent's informed consent.
  • It misrepresents to the service provider that you have the right to submit the photo.

Tale With You Terms of Service v1.3, Article 12, requires the ordering person to declare that they are the parent or legal guardian of the child in the photos. Article 19 specifies that photos must be received via Shopee chat or LINE; if the brand has reasonable doubt that the submitter is not the parent or guardian, it may refuse production or cancel the order.

3. The correct order of steps

  1. Propose the gift first. Contact the friend: "I'd like to give [child's name] a custom picture book. Would your family be comfortable with that?"
  2. Let the parent decide. Give them enough information to decide: what it involves (photos, an AI illustration book), which brand, a link to the privacy policy.
  3. Let the parent read the privacy policy. Do not ask them to just trust you. Send the link to the privacy policy directly. If they have questions, they can contact the brand.
  4. Let the parent submit the photos via the official channel. Do not ask the parent to send the photos to you first so you can submit them. The parent should contact the brand directly via Shopee or LINE and submit photos there.
  5. Payment can be handled separately. You pay for the order; the parent handles the photos and the preview review. Many parents will appreciate taking the preview step themselves.

FAQ

Q1: Can I order a custom picture book as a gift for a friend's child?
Yes, but you cannot submit the child's photos yourself. The child's parent or legal guardian must submit the photos—and must be informed of the purpose and consent before doing so. You can pay for the gift; the parent handles the photo steps.
Q2: Can I use photos of my friend's child that they posted on social media?
No. Using a photo from social media submits it to a third-party AI and cloud processor without the parent's informed consent for that specific purpose—different from why they originally shared it. Tale With You's terms require the submitter to declare they are the parent or legal guardian; the brand may refuse if there is reasonable doubt.
Q3: What if I want it to be a surprise for the parent too?
A complete surprise is not feasible for a custom book using a child's photos. The parent must actively submit the photos and review the preview. The gift can still be meaningful if framed as 'I want to give your child this, and I'd like your help making it—I'll pay for it.'
