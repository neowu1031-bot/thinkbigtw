Giving a Custom Book to a Friend's Child: How Do You Get the Photos, and Who Do You Ask First?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-08   ·  中文

The short answer

Ordering a custom picture book as a gift for a friend's child requires the consent and participation of the child's parents or guardians. The photo used in the book is personal data; under Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, the person providing personal data must consent, and for a minor, consent must be given by a parent or legal guardian. When a third party orders, the person placing the order is not the data subject's guardian, so the child's parent must be involved in uploading the photo and confirming the preview.

1. Why the child's parents must be involved

Under Civil Code Article 1086, parents are the legal representatives of a minor child. For a child's photo—which is personal data under the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) Article 2—the person who can decide how to use it is the child's parent or guardian, not a third party.

PDPA Article 7 requires that the collection of personal data be with the data subject's consent; Article 8 requires that the collecting party notify the data subject of the purpose, categories, period, territory, recipients, and methods of use, as well as the data subject's rights. For a minor, the parent or guardian gives this consent. So even if you want to give a gift, you cannot simply take the child's photo and place an order without the parents knowing. (Personal Data Protection Act, Civil Code)

2. How to handle it in practice

Step 1: Notify the child's parents first. Tell them you want to give this gift and ask if they are willing to cooperate. Don't collect photos before asking.

Step 2: Let the parents provide the photo. Photos should come from the child's parent directly, not from group photos or social media. The parents should read the provider's privacy policy and photo usage instructions.

Step 3: Let the parents confirm the preview. The preview is a low-resolution watermarked image of the cover and some interior pages. The parents should confirm the character's appearance, the child's name and nickname, and the dedication text. Any adjustments should be communicated through the parents.

Step 4: Coordinate the delivery address. You can pay, but the delivery address can be to the recipient's family or to your own address for forwarding; decide in advance and inform the parents.

3. What about providers' own rules

Some providers specify that only the child's parents or guardians may place orders and upload photos. Check the provider's terms of service before ordering to confirm whether third-party ordering is accepted and what the specific process is.

FAQ

Q1: Can I order a custom picture book as a gift for a friend's child?
You can pay, but the child's parent or guardian must be involved in uploading the photo and confirming the preview. Under the Personal Data Protection Act, the person who can consent to the use of a child's photo is the parent or guardian.
Q2: Can I use a photo from a group photo or social media?
It is not recommended. Photos should come directly from the child's parent; it is best to let the parent select the photo and read the provider's privacy policy before providing it.
Q3: Why can't a third party place the order on behalf of the child?
Under PDPA Articles 7 and 8, the collection of personal data requires the data subject's consent and the fulfillment of notification obligations; for a minor, the parent or guardian gives consent. A third-party orderer is not the guardian and cannot substitute for the parent's consent.
Q4: Does Tale With You accept orders from friends as gifts?
Tale With You's Terms of Service requires that the orderer be the parent or guardian of the child in the photos, or have obtained their authorization. It is recommended to coordinate with the parents first; the parents should place the order, upload the photo, and confirm the preview.
