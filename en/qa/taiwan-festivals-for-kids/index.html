How to Talk to Children About Taiwan's Festivals: Shared-Reading Approaches for 7 Holidays

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-29   ·  中文

The short answer

When talking about festivals with children, starting with "how does our family celebrate" is the easiest approach: first ask what the child remembers, then look at the calendar to find the date, and finally talk about the festival's origins and customs. For dates, the Commemorative Days and Holidays Implementation Regulations can be consulted: Chinese New Year (Spring Festival), Lantern Festival, Qingming, Dragon Boat Festival, Mid-Autumn Festival, and New Year's Eve are all listed as folk holidays; National Day is a commemorative day. Among these, New Year's Eve and Spring Festival have 5 days off; Qingming, Dragon Boat Festival, and Mid-Autumn Festival each have 1 day off; National Day has 1 day off; the Lantern Festival has no holiday; and the Winter Solstice is not listed among the holidays in the Regulations.

1. 7 festivals — what the regulations say

The Commemorative Days and Holidays Implementation Regulations, promulgated and implemented on 28 May 2025, list commemorative days in Article 3, folk holidays in Article 5, and holidays in Articles 4 and 6 (Laws and Regulations Database):

When a holiday falls on a day off, a makeup day is provided (Article 8). Article 5 of the Regulations also lists Ghost Festival (Zhongyuan), Double Ninth Festival (Chongyang), and Indigenous People's harvest festivals as folk holidays; Article 3 also includes National Hakka Day (December 28). Taiwan has more than these 7 festivals; how your family celebrates is something to choose from your own customs.

2. How the curriculum framework views festivals

The Ministry of Education's Preschool Curriculum Framework lists "people and the environment" as a learning dimension in the social domain, with content including understanding the various roles and activities of people in the community, cultural customs and festivals, and diverse ways of living; the curriculum objectives also include "understanding cultural diversity in the living environment." (Ministry of Education Legal System) For children, festivals are a window into understanding "how other families and our family celebrate."

3. 4 ways to talk about festivals

The following are practical suggestions, not content from the regulations or curriculum framework:

1. Start by asking what the child remembers. "Last Chinese New Year, whose house did we go to? What did we eat?" Starting from the child's own experience is easier to connect with than explaining origins first.

2. Look at the calendar together and find the date. The folk holidays in the Regulations mostly use the lunar calendar; National Day uses the solar calendar. Circle them on the calendar — the child will discover that some holidays fall on different solar calendar dates each year.

3. Start with what to eat and what to do. For each festival, pick one thing the child can experience: what to eat, what to do, where to go — these are easier to remember than reciting origins.

4. Let the child tell "how our family celebrates." Every family celebrates the same festival differently; encourage the child to tell family members or classmates — this is what the curriculum framework means by understanding cultural diversity.

FAQ

Q1: What are Taiwan's folk holidays?
Under Article 5 of the Commemorative Days and Holidays Implementation Regulations, the folk holidays are: Spring Festival, Lantern Festival, Qingming Festival, Dragon Boat Festival, Ghost Festival (Zhongyuan), Mid-Autumn Festival, Double Ninth Festival (Chongyang), New Year's Eve, and Indigenous People's harvest festivals.
Q2: Is the Lantern Festival a public holiday?
No. Article 6 of the Commemorative Days and Holidays Implementation Regulations specifies the holidays with time off — New Year's Eve and Spring Festival (5 days), Children's Day, Qingming Festival, Labor Day, Dragon Boat Festival, Teachers' Day, Mid-Autumn Festival (1 day each), and so on; the Lantern Festival is not among them.
Q3: Is the Winter Solstice an official holiday?
Neither Article 3's commemorative days nor Article 5's folk holidays in the Commemorative Days and Holidays Implementation Regulations list the Winter Solstice, so it is not a holiday under the Regulations and there is no day off.
