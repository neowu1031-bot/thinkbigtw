How to Talk to Children About Taiwan's Festivals: Shared-Reading Approaches for 7 Holidays

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-29   ·  中文

The short answer

Taiwan's Commemorative Days and Festivals Implementation Act (published May 28, 2025) defines which days are official festivals. For preschoolers, the goal is not memorizing dates but connecting the festival to things the child can perceive: food, activities, and how the family celebrates. Taiwan's Ministry of Education Preschool Curriculum Guidelines include 'cultural customs and festivals' in the social domain, specifically noting that children should understand them through their own and their family's lived experience, not through abstract explanation.

1. Seven festivals at a glance

Under Taiwan's Commemorative Days and Festivals Implementation Act (published May 28, 2025), the following are official festivals (稿穌日):

Note: Winter Solstice is a traditional festival widely observed but not listed as an official festival under the Act; it is not a national holiday. Ghost Festival, Double Ninth, and Indigenous harvest ceremonies are also listed in the Act.

2. Curriculum context

Taiwan's Ministry of Education Preschool Curriculum Guidelines social domain includes "people and environment" as a learning dimension, with cultural customs and festivals explicitly mentioned as content. The approach recommended in the guidelines is experiential and dialogue-based—children learn about their culture through participating, asking questions, and hearing family stories, not through lecture.

3. Four conversation approaches for any festival

1. Start with the child's memories. "Do you remember what we did for Dragon Boat Festival last year?" Before explaining the festival, find out what the child already knows and feels. This is the basis of the experiential approach the curriculum recommends.

2. Look at the calendar together. Point to when the festival is coming. "It is in ___ days. That's how long we have to wait." Connecting the festival to perceived time makes it real, not abstract.

3. Start with food and activities, not history. "What do we eat for this festival?" is a better opening than "In ancient China..." For preschoolers, the food, colors, and activities are the festival. History can follow once the experience is anchored.

4. Let the child explain it to someone else. When grandparents visit or a younger sibling asks, let the older child explain what Dragon Boat Festival is in their own words. Teaching consolidates understanding and builds pride in cultural knowledge.

FAQ

Q1: What are Taiwan's official festivals?
Under the Commemorative Days and Festivals Implementation Act (published May 28, 2025), the official festivals include Spring Festival, Lantern Festival, Tomb Sweeping, Dragon Boat Festival, Mid-Autumn Festival, National Day, and others. Winter Solstice is widely observed but not listed as an official festival under the Act.
Q2: How do I explain Taiwan's festivals to a preschooler?
Start with their memories, look at the calendar together, begin with food and activities rather than history, and let them explain it to someone else. Taiwan's Preschool Curriculum Guidelines recommend an experiential, dialogue-based approach—not lecture.
Q3: Is Winter Solstice an official holiday in Taiwan?
No. Winter Solstice (Dongzhi) is not listed as an official festival in the Commemorative Days and Festivals Implementation Act and is not a national holiday. It is widely observed as a traditional family occasion.
