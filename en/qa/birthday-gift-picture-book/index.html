Shared Reading & Custom Picture Books Q10

How to Pick a Birthday Gift Book a Child Will Read Again and Again

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-20   ·  中文

The short answer

A birthday gift book that gets read again and again usually has two things: it matches what the child is genuinely interested in right now, and there is a trusted adult willing to read it together. The toy safety checklist from Taiwan's Bureau of Standards, Metrology and Inspection (BSMI) applies to books that come with physical components (toys, dolls); for a plain picture book, the main questions are whether the content is age-appropriate and whether the production quality is durable enough for repeated handling.

1. Taiwan BSMI toy safety: when it applies to books

A book with no attached physical toys or components is not classified as a toy under Taiwan BSMI regulations and does not require the commodity inspection mark (倪補工業宣典测試无悪儲補読笔子、笔富設料) for books alone. However, if a custom picture book is sold bundled with stuffed animals, toys, or small figurines, those components must meet BSMI standards and carry the commodity inspection mark.

BSMI (September 28, 2023) recommends 7 toy safety checks: look for the commodity inspection mark, check the Chinese label and stated age range, choose age-appropriate products, do not let infants chew on small-parts toys, check structural integrity (no sharp edges), no magnetic toys for infants, and discard plastic packaging bags immediately.

2. What makes a birthday book truly last

The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2024 policy statement in Pediatrics emphasizes that reading together from infancy strengthens the parent-child bond and builds language and literacy foundations. Research also found children more engaged with books where they were the main character (see Do Books Starring Your Child Really Help With Reading?).

Practically, a birthday book that keeps being read tends to have:

  • Content the child currently loves. Dinosaurs, vehicles, a favorite animal—a book about something they are passionate about right now gets opened again the next day.
  • Durable construction. A sewn or perfectly-bound spine survives far more opens-and-closes than a saddle-stitched stapled spine. For gifting, check how the book is bound.
  • An adult willing to read it. Even the best book sits unread if no one reads it with the child. For a gift, make clear to the parents that this is a book to read together—not just display.

3. Ordering as a gift for someone else's child

If you are buying a custom picture book as a gift for someone else's child, you cannot submit the child's photos yourself—that must be done by the child's parent or legal guardian. See Giving a Custom Book to a Friend's Child: How Do You Get the Photos, and Who Do You Ask First?

FAQ

Q1: What should I look for when choosing a birthday picture book as a gift?
Match the child's current interests; check the binding is durable enough for repeated reading; and make sure there is an adult willing to sit and read it together. The AAP's 2024 policy emphasizes reading together as the key to language and literacy development.
Q2: Do BSMI toy safety rules apply to picture books?
Plain picture books with no attached physical components are not classified as toys; the BSMI commodity inspection mark requirement applies to toys. If the book is bundled with stuffed animals or figurines, those components must meet BSMI standards.
Q3: Can I order a custom picture book as a birthday gift for someone else's child?
Yes, but the child's parent or legal guardian must be the one who submits the child's photos. You can propose and pay for the gift; the parent handles the photos and reviews the preview. See the full guide in 'Giving a Custom Book to a Friend's Child.'
