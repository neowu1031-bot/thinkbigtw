Do Bilingual Picture Books Help Children? How to Read Them Together at Home

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-25   ·  中文

The short answer

Bilingual picture books may be helpful, but the existing evidence is limited. A randomized study had 22 Spanish-speaking children aged 4–6 who were learning English receive two types of word explanations during book reading, 2 weeks each; in the weeks with home-language (Spanish) explanations, children learned more vocabulary than in the weeks with English-only explanations. However, the study was small, the language combination was not Chinese-English, and it cannot be directly used as evidence that Chinese-English bilingual picture books are definitely effective. At home, start with the language the child is familiar with; add English sentences as supplementary.

1. What one study found

Lugo-Neris, Jackson, and Goldstein (2010) studied 22 Spanish-speaking children aged 4–6 who were learning English. During weeks when explanations were given in Spanish (their home language), children acquired more English vocabulary than during weeks when explanations were given in English. The researchers concluded that using the home language to explain book content can support second-language vocabulary learning. (Language, Speech, and Hearing Services in Schools, 2010)

2. What this study did not say

The sample was small. The study had 22 children, all Spanish-speaking preschoolers learning English in the United States. The findings should not be directly applied to all language combinations or family environments.

The language environment is different. Taiwan's situation—Mandarin as the home language, English as a foreign language—is different from the study's context. Whether the same findings apply needs further examination.

3. What Taiwan's 2030 Bilingual Policy says

The Executive Yuan's 2030 Bilingual Policy aims for citizens to be able to use both Mandarin and English in their daily lives and work; it does not mandate at what age children must start or which method parents should use. (Executive Yuan 2030 Bilingual Policy)

The AAP's 2024 policy statement in Pediatrics encourages parents to read and converse with children from infancy in the language they are most comfortable with; it also affirms that growing up bilingual does not cause language delays. (Pediatrics 2024)

4. 4 practical steps

1. Use the language you are more comfortable with. The AAP's recommendation is to read in the language you are most comfortable with; a smooth and natural read makes for better shared reading.

2. Try the home-language explanation method. Point to the picture, name the item in English, then say it once more in Mandarin. This follows the direction of the study: explaining in the home language may help the child understand the new language word.

3. Don't require the child to respond in a specific language. Let the child respond in whichever language they like; don't interrupt the story to correct their language.

4. Repeat frequently. Repetition is the key to vocabulary acquisition; reading the same bilingual book several times is more effective than briefly reading many books.

5. Limitations to keep in mind

This article is based primarily on one study; the findings are preliminary and should not be treated as a definitive conclusion. Family language environment, the child's age, and exposure time all affect results. If you have specific concerns about bilingual development, it is recommended to consult a speech-language pathologist or related specialist.

FAQ

Q1: Can reading bilingual picture books help children learn English?
The confirmable evidence is limited. A randomized study showed that when home-language (Spanish) word explanations were used during English book reading, children learned more vocabulary than English-only; but the study only had 22 children, and the language combination was Spanish-English, so it cannot be directly applied to Chinese-English.
Q2: How should I read Chinese-English bilingual books at home?
Start by discussing the story in the language the child is familiar with; add English sentences as supplementary—one sentence per page, pointing at the pictures; skip the English if the child is not interested; keep the order consistent.
Q3: What does Taiwan's 2030 Bilingual Policy require of parents?
The 2030 Bilingual Policy's goal is for citizens to be able to use both Mandarin and English in daily life and work; it does not mandate at what age children must start bilingual learning or which method parents should use.
Q4: Does Tale With You offer an English option?
Yes. The English option is a rhyming English sentence per page, not a word-for-word translation of the Chinese text; it is optional and chosen at order time.
