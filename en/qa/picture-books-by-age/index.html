Shared Reading & Custom Picture Books Q14

What Kind of Picture Books Suit Each Stage from 0 to 5 Years Old?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-24   ·  中文

The short answer

Picture books can be chosen according to a child's developmental stage: ages 0–6 months suit toy books and cloth books with vivid colors and large patterns; ages 6–12 months suit thick board books that are easy to hold and turn; ages 1–2 suit books with large illustrations and few words that relate to everyday life; ages 2–3 suit stories with simple plots and repetitive sentences; ages 3–5 add humorous, emotional, and informational books. This is a summary from the Ministry of Education's Bookstart handbook; age is only a reference — the child's interest and their mood that day matter more.

1. How to choose for each stage

Since 2009 (Year 98 of the Republic of China), the Ministry of Education has promoted Bookstart (閱讀起步走), encouraging parent-child shared reading for children ages 0–5, and commissioned the National Taiwan Library to select outstanding books each year and compile reading handbooks; recommended booklists and handbooks from each year can be found on the National Taiwan Library's Bookstart page. The table below is adapted from the "Infant and Toddler Development Characteristics and Shared Reading Strategy Reference Table" in the appendix of the Year 110 (2021) handbook (Bookstart reading handbook), rewritten in everyday language.

0–6 monthsLong sleep periods; blurry vision but can track movement; likes looking at faces; acute hearing and touch.Toy books with vivid colors, simple outlines, large patterns, and special shapes; cloth books with different textures that can be grasped and squeezed.Sing lullabies and nursery rhymes while caring for the baby; hold and talk to the baby often; place pictures or toys with strong color contrast near the sleeping area.
6–12 monthsGradually able to sit; starting to crawl; likes grabbing things and will try to take and turn pages; puts things in their mouth; notices patterns in books and pats them; can distinguish different speech sounds.Cloth books and bath books; picture books with short words and simple patterns; sturdy, easy-to-turn board books; interactive play books.Use vocal variation or small sound-making props to play story games together; let the child freely touch books and guide them to learn page-turning; point to illustrations and name them during shared reading.
1–2 yearsStarting to learn to talk; can stand and walk; short attention span, easily distracted; can hold books and turn pages forward and back.Picture books related to daily experience (food, toys); picture books with vivid colors, large illustrations, and few words; play books.During bath or hygiene routines, chant body-part songs with matching actions; let the child be in charge of turning pages, or use a playful approach to tell the story with varied vocal expressions and body language.
2–3 yearsMore vocabulary and sentence patterns; loves asking "what's that?"; beginning to understand simple cause and effect; will repeatedly read the same book.Picture books with simple plots and repetitive sentence patterns; content based on the child's daily routines and habits, such as eating, bathing, brushing teeth, using the toilet, sleeping, and visiting the doctor.Slow down when telling the story; pay attention to whether sentence length is easy for the child to remember and imitate.

2. 3 things to remember

1. Age is a reference, not a threshold. The table shows common characteristics for each stage; the child's interest and state today are more accurate than the age column.

2. Shared reading can continue after age 5. The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2024 policy statement recommends that shared reading begin at birth and continue at least through kindergarten. (Pediatrics 2024) The source handbook covers ages 0–5, so stages after age 5 are not listed in this article.

3. Choose books that the adults themselves find interesting. The handbook's shared reading FAQ notes that if even the adult finds it boring, it's hard to muster the energy to read it to the child.

FAQ

Q1: What picture books are suitable for children aged 0–3?
According to the Ministry of Education's Bookstart handbook: ages 0–6 months suit toy books and cloth books with vivid colors and large patterns; ages 6–12 months suit easy-to-hold board books; ages 1–2 suit picture books with large illustrations and few words that relate to daily life; ages 2–3 suit stories with simple plots, repetitive sentence patterns, and content based on daily experience.
Q2: What picture books are suitable for children aged 3–5?
According to the Ministry of Education's Bookstart handbook, ages 3–5 suit humorous books, books about emotions, life stories that broaden experience, stories about family affection and friendship, and informational picture books; during shared reading, children can be guided to recognize the correspondence between characters and sounds, and encouraged to tell stories to family members.
Q3: At what age is shared reading no longer necessary?
There is no such age. The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2024 policy statement recommends that shared reading begin at birth and continue at least through kindergarten.
