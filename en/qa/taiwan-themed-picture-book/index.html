Is There a Taiwan-Themed Picture Book Starring Your Child? Using One Book to Explore Taiwan

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-14   ·  中文

The short answer

Yes. Among custom picture books that star the child, there are already Taiwan-themed options. Tale With You's 'Taiwan Island Tour' puts the child in scenes set in Taipei, Keelung, Jiufen, Hualien, Kenting, Alishan, and the Penghu Islands; a second theme, 'Taiwan Festival Fun', follows a year of traditional festivals. To help children truly remember these places, the most effective approach is to connect the book pages with a map and real outings.

1. Why putting the child "in" Taiwan's scenery works

Children are especially engaged by content that relates to them personally. Research found that 3-year-olds learned new words faster in sections of a book where they were the main character (see Do Books Starring Your Child Really Help With Reading?). Place names, vehicles, and local specialties are more memorable when placed in the context of "I took the train to Hualien" rather than listed on a geography sheet.

Taiwan-themed books have another advantage: the places in the book might be reachable on a weekend trip. When the book connects to the real world, children will keep returning to it.

2. A "tour-around-Taiwan" reading map you can make at home

1. Get a map of Taiwan. A large poster or an A4 printout both work. Each time the book visits a destination, let the child place a small sticker on the map.

2. Ask one question per stop. "How do we get to Penghu?" (by boat) "What do you ride to get to Jiufen?" You do not need to cover everything at once; one question per stop is enough.

3. Add one real fact per stop. Use publicly available information as source material so you can confirm it if the child asks:

  • Kenting: Declared a national park in the 71st year of the ROC calendar (1982)—Taiwan's first national park; the management office was established in year 73 (1984). Yushan, Yangmingshan, Taroko, and others followed. (Executive Yuan Country Brief)
  • Alishan: The Alishan Forest Railway was out of service for years after Typhoon Morakot and resumed full-line service on July 6, 2024—after a 15-year gap; Alishan Station sits at 2,216 m above sea level. (Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency, Ministry of Agriculture)

4. Bring the book on the actual outing. When you arrive somewhere that appears in the book, find an angle that matches the page and take a photo. Placing the photo next to the book page at home gives children a real sense of accomplishment.

3. From geography to festivals

Knowing Taiwan is not only about "where to go" but also "how people live". New Year's Eve reunion dinner, Lantern Festival lanterns, Dragon Boat Festival racing, Mid-Autumn Moon Festival: these holidays come around every year and are well-suited to reading about the week before the holiday arrives, then experiencing them in person.
