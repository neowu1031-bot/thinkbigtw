Is There a Taiwan-Themed Picture Book Starring Your Child? Using One Book to Explore Taiwan

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-14   ·  中文

The short answer

Yes. Custom picture books where the child is the main character already include Taiwan-themed options — for example, Tale With You's "Taiwan Island Tour" places the child (in their own likeness) in book pages featuring Taipei, Keelung, Jiufen, Hualien, Kenting, Alishan, and Penghu; the "Taiwan Festivals" theme follows a year of festivals. To help children truly remember these places, the most effective approach is to connect the book pages with maps and real outings.

1. Why put the child "in" Taiwan's scenery

Children engage more with content that relates to themselves. Research has found that 3-year-old children learn new words faster in book passages where they are the main character (see Do books where the child is the main character really help with reading?). Place names, vehicles, and local specialties placed in the context of "I took the train to Hualien" are easier to remember than on a list in a textbook.

Taiwan-themed books have another advantage: the places in the book might be somewhere you can actually visit the next weekend. When the book and the real world connect, the child will keep coming back to it.

2. An "island-tour reading map" you can make at home

1. Get a map of Taiwan. A large one or an A4 printout works. Each time you read about a stop, let the child place a small sticker on the map.

2. Ask only one question per stop. "How do we get to Penghu?" (by boat), "What do we take to get to Jiufen?" — no need to explain a lot at once; one question per stop is enough.

3. Add one real fact per stop. Use publicly available government information as your material, so when the child asks "really?" you can answer. For example:

  • Kenting: Established in Year 71 of the Republic of China (1982), it is Taiwan's first national park; its management office was established in Year 73. Other national parks including Yushan, Yangmingshan, and Taroko followed. (Executive Yuan national profile)
  • Alishan: The Alishan Forest Railway was out of service for many years following Typhoon Morakot; after 15 years, it restored full-line service on 6 July 2024; Alishan Station sits at an elevation of 2,216 meters. (Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency, Ministry of Agriculture)

4. Bring the book on real outings. When you reach a place that has appeared in the book, find an angle similar to the book page and take a photo. Back home, place the photo next to the book — the child will feel a great sense of achievement.

3. From geography to festivals

Knowing Taiwan means not just "where to go" but also "how people live." New Year's Eve reunion dinner, Lantern Festival parade, Dragon Boat Festival race, Mid-Autumn Festival moon-gazing — these festivals come around every year, and reading about them in the week before each one, then actually celebrating them, makes the connection complete.

FAQ

Q1: Is there a Taiwan-themed picture book where the child is the main character?
Yes. Tale With You's "Taiwan Island Tour" theme places the child (in their own likeness) in book pages featuring Taipei, Keelung, Jiufen, Hualien, Kenting, Alishan, and Penghu; the "Taiwan Festivals" theme follows a year of festivals from New Year's Eve reunion dinner to Winter Solstice tangyuan.
Q2: How can a book be used to introduce Taiwan's geography to children?
Get a map of Taiwan; each time you read about a stop, place a small sticker on the map; ask only one question per stop and add one real fact; bring the book on actual outings and photograph the same angle as the book page.
Q3: When was Kenting National Park established?
According to the Executive Yuan national profile, Kenting National Park was established by proclamation in Year 71 of the Republic of China (1982) and is Taiwan's first national park; its management office was established in Year 73.
Q4: Can the Alishan Forest Railway's full route be ridden now?
Yes. According to material from the Forestry and Nature Conservation Agency, the Alishan Forest Railway restored full-line service on 6 July 2024, after 15 years out of service following Typhoon Morakot.
