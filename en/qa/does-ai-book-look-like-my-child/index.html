Does an AI Custom Book Look Like My Child? Why You Should Check the Preview Before Printing

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-09   ·  中文

The short answer

AI custom picture book illustrations may differ from the actual person, so before printing you should check your own preview rather than relying only on the sample characters on the website. Using Tale With You's description as an example, illustrations are drawn referencing the photos, and a preview is sent before printing; parents can first check the character, name, and dedication, then request adjustments according to the service's rules. How many pages are in the preview and whether changes are still possible after confirmation should be asked in advance.

1. Turn "doesn't look like" into specific details that can be discussed

Tale With You's FAQ explains that the main character is generated based on the photos, with the face, hair, and skin tone checked page by page; it also notes that illustrations may not be completely identical to the person. This is the brand's description of its production method, not an objective score of how similar each finished book will be.

When reviewing the preview, you can first write down the features you care about: the direction of the fringe, hair length, glasses, or face shape. If something seems off, it is recommended to use a specific description such as "the original photo shows hair to below the ear, but the preview shows it reaching the shoulder," and then ask the provider whether it can be addressed. These are examples of communication; they do not mean each item can be revised individually.

2. Review the preview in three rounds, don't rush to send confirmation

What to reviewRecommended approach
CharacterPlace the original photo beside it and first check the features you care about. If an adult also appears, have that person check their own illustration too.
TextRead the name, nickname, and dedication aloud word by word to check for any incorrect characters or wording that is not what the family intended.
Story and visualsConfirm whether the pages provided match the chosen theme; note any issues such as extra limbs or illegible text, and ask about each one.

It is recommended that one parent consolidate the feedback into a single message: "The issues I see are…, and I would like to confirm the range of adjustments that are possible…." If only some pages are shown in the preview, ask who checks the remaining pages and how issues with pages not shown are handled. Do not assume on your own that "having a preview" means every page can be reviewed individually.

Tale With You's Terms of Service Article 4 states that the preview is the cover and some interior pages; the name and dedication text may be revised before confirmation; each order may regenerate the entire book once at no charge. This rule is a full regeneration, not unlimited individual page revisions.

3. Also ask about the scope and method of confirmation

The brand's terms state that once "confirmed" is replied, the order enters printing and the content cannot be changed afterward. Therefore, if there are any questions, you can first write "I still want to confirm something here, please don't send to print yet," and wait for the provider's reply before deciding. This is a communication approach recommended in this article; the actual process is subject to the provider's confirmation.

It is also recommended to keep the confirmed images and text in an order record you can find, and to check with family members before sharing. According to the brand's Privacy Policy, previews are low-resolution files with watermarks; the provider's own files and messages on platforms have different deletion scopes; do not assume the other party will keep all versions for you long-term.

For improving photo preparation, see How to take a clear photo; for remaining questions about revisions, cancellations, and delivery times, first check the pre-purchase checklist.

If family members have differing opinions, it is recommended to first list the items you all care about, then ask about the scope of possible adjustments, to avoid missing any issues across multiple messages.

FAQ

Q1: If the character doesn't look like the child, how do I describe it clearly?
You can describe the specific differences between the original photo and the preview, then ask whether an adjustment is possible, rather than just writing "doesn't look like."
Q2: If only the cover is received, does that count as reviewing the whole book?
No. You should confirm the scope of the preview, how the pages not provided are checked, and how issues with pages not shown are handled.
Q3: Should I ask the child to rate the preview?
Not necessary. You can invite the child to look at the picture, but do not ask them to say it looks like them or that they are satisfied; the text and process of the order are confirmed by the parent.
Q4: Does Tale With You's regeneration mean individual page revisions?
The terms state that each order may regenerate the entire book once at no charge; revisions to the name and dedication text are unlimited before confirmation.
