Is AI Speaking Practice Safe for Children? 4 Questions Parents Can Ask First

Think BIG Q&A Library　·　November 12, 2026　·　中文版

Short answer

Whether AI speaking practice is safe depends on how the service handles a child's voice and data, and whether an adult is involved, so no blanket answer is possible. The US COPPA Rule treats audio files containing a child's voice as personal information, and UNESCO's 2023 guidance mentioned considering an age limit of 13 for generative AI in classrooms. Parents can first ask: is the voice kept, who gets it, how is it deleted, and can I join in.

1.Why voice needs a special question

Speaking practice necessarily captures a child's voice. The US Federal Trade Commission's COPPA FAQ states that photos, videos and audio recordings containing a child's image or voice are personal information and generally need verifiable parental consent. The FTC also describes a conditional non-enforcement policy for audio used only as a replacement for typing: the operator may not use voice to ask for information that is itself personal (such as a name) and must post a clear notice of how the recordings are collected, used and deleted. (FTC: COPPA FAQ F.1 and F.6)

2.Age thresholds: what reference exists

In September 2023 UNESCO issued guidance on generative AI in education, called for regulation, and mentioned considering an age limit of 13 for classroom use. (UN News, 7 Sep 2023) It concerns generative AI in school classrooms, not language-learning apps specifically, so treat it as a reference only.

3.Four questions to ask

QuestionWhy ask
1. Is my child's voice stored?Stored recordings may be personal information (see above).
2. Who processes the voice or conversation?Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, Article 8, requires disclosure of the recipients, area and manner of use. (Laws and Regulations Database (Chinese))
3. How can I view and delete it?Article 3 protects the rights to inquire, access and request deletion; they cannot be waived in advance.
4. Can I join in?Practising together makes problems easier to notice than leaving a child alone with a tool (a parenting practice, not a legal requirement).

Frequently asked questions

Q1: Is AI speaking practice safe for children?
It depends on how the service handles the child's voice and data and whether an adult is involved. Ask: is the voice kept, who processes it, how can it be viewed and deleted, and can a parent join in.
Q2: Is a child's voice recording personal information?
Under the US COPPA Rule, audio files containing a child's voice are personal information (FTC FAQ F.6). Under Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, the test is whether the data can directly or indirectly identify a person.
Q3: What age is suitable for AI tools?
There is no single legal answer. UNESCO's 2023 guidance mentioned considering an age limit of 13 for generative AI in classrooms. That is a school reference, not a rule for language apps.
