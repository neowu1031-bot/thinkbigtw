Sharing Your Child's Photo for a Custom Picture Book: 6 Privacy Questions to Ask First

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-12   ·  中文

The short answer

Before handing over your child's photo to a custom picture book service, ask six things: what the photo will be used for, whether it will be used to train AI, which companies will handle it (and whether it crosses borders), how it is stored, when it will be deleted (and what cannot be deleted), and how to request access or deletion. Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act, Article 8, requires providers to disclose these details when collecting personal data; Article 3 guarantees your right to inquire, correct, stop processing, and request deletion, and those rights cannot be pre-waived by contract.

1. Why children's photos need extra care

A clear frontal photo of a child can directly identify who the person is. The Personal Data Protection Act, Article 2, includes "characteristics" and "other data that can directly or indirectly identify an individual" as personal data (Laws & Regulations Database), so a photo uploaded for a custom picture book is covered by the Act from the moment it is submitted.

A second reason is that children cannot decide for themselves. The parent is placing the order, but the child is in the photo. When evaluating a service, thinking "if this photo were to leak, would my child mind when they grow up?" helps you notice questions you might otherwise skip.

Taiwan's Basic Act on Artificial Intelligence, Article 4, promulgated January 14, 2026, lists "privacy protection and data governance" as one of the principles governing AI development, requiring proper protection of personal data and use of data minimization—collecting and using only what is necessary (Laws & Regulations Database). This can serve as a reference standard when selecting an AI-based custom service.

2. Six questions to ask before placing an order

A reputable provider should be able to answer all six questions in their privacy policy. If the answers are missing or vague, ask before you order.

3. Three things parents can do themselves

1. Provide only the photos necessary. Make sure the photo contains only your child, not other children; give as many photos as the provider requires, not more.

2. Send photos only through the provider's designated official channel. Do not post your child's photo in public comments or on social media.

3. After receiving the book, follow up on deletion. Compare against the provider's stated deletion timeline; to request early deletion, you may invoke your rights under PDPA Article 3.

FAQ

Q1: Is it safe to use my child's photo for a custom picture book?
Whether it is safe depends on how the provider handles the photo. Before ordering, confirm six things: the photo's purpose, whether it will be used for AI training, which companies will handle it, how it is stored, when it will be deleted, and how to request access or deletion. These are disclosures required by PDPA Article 8.
Q2: If the provider says the photo will be deleted, does that mean everything is gone?
Not necessarily. Common exceptions: photos in messaging app or shopping platform chat rooms (retained by the platform); data the AI service provider retains under its own policy; legally required invoice and order records. A good privacy policy states these exceptions explicitly.
Q3: Can I ask the provider to delete my child's photo?
Yes. PDPA Article 3 guarantees the data subject's rights to inquire, review, obtain copies, correct, stop collection and processing, and request deletion, and these rights cannot be pre-waived by contract.
Q4: Does Tale With You use children's photos to train AI?
No. Per Tale With You Privacy Policy v1.3, photos are used only to produce that one book and are not used to train any AI model; the OpenAI account used has the 'Improve the model for everyone' setting turned off. All files are deleted within three days of confirmed delivery.
