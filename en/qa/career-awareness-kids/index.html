My Child Says They Want to Be Something When They Grow Up: How to Nurture Career Curiosity

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-01   ·  中文

The short answer

Starting career awareness for preschoolers can begin with two steps: "picture books first, then try it out." Read picture books about occupations first to let the child come into contact with more types of jobs; then find one job in their daily environment to observe or experience. The National Academy for Educational Research's 2020 Career Education Handbook states that career awareness at the preschool stage is about exposure and experience, not training for a future job.

1. What the curriculum framework says

The Ministry of Education's Preschool Curriculum Framework (social domain) lists "understanding people and environments" as a learning dimension, including understanding people's roles and activities, cultural customs and festivals, and diverse ways of living in the community. Occupations are one type of people's role. The curriculum objective is to "recognize the diverse phenomena of culture in the living environment"—not to prepare for a particular career. (Ministry of Education Legal Information)

The National Academy for Educational Research's Career Education Implementation Handbook (2020) states that career awareness at the preschool stage focuses on exposure and experience, allowing children to understand what occupations exist through daily life, play, and stories, rather than developing specific skills. (NAER Handbook)

2. Picture books first, then try it out

Step 1: Choose picture books about daily occupations. Doctors, bakers, train drivers, farmers—any occupation the child encounters in daily life is worth reading about. After reading, ask "what did this person do today?" to help the child organize what they learned.

Step 2: Find one job in the living environment to observe. The delivery person who comes to the house, the person at the community convenience store, the cleaner at the park—these are people with jobs that the child can actually observe. After observing, connect it back to the picture book: "This is similar to the baker in the book, right?"

Step 3: Let the child try it out in play. NAER's handbook points to play as one of the main ways preschoolers experience occupations. Set up a "store" at home, let the child be the cook, let toys be customers. Trying it out is more effective for understanding than explaining.

3. What not to rush

Career awareness at the preschool stage is "this occupation exists, this person does this kind of work." Asking "what do you want to be when you grow up?" is a question to leave for later; answering it too early may cause children to narrow their range of exploration. The NAER handbook also emphasizes letting children freely explore different types of work rather than guiding them toward a specific answer.
