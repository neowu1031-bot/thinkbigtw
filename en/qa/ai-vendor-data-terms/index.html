Before Adopting an AI Service, Which Points in the Vendor's Data Terms Should You Check?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-29   ·  中文

The short answer

Before signing, check at least six points: what the data is used for (including whether it trains models), how long it is kept, which country or region it is stored in, which downstream vendors receive it (subcontracting), how soon you are notified if something goes wrong, and how data is returned or deleted when the contract ends. Most of these map to Article 8 of the Enforcement Rules of Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act: when a company commissions someone else to process personal data, its oversight must at least cover the scope, categories, purpose, and period of processing; the contractor's security measures; subcontracting; notification and remedies for violations; and return and deletion when the commission ends. And what matters is the plan and contract you actually use, not the brand's general description.

1. Six points to check

Point What to look for in the terms Basis
1. What the data is used for Whether it trains or improves models, whether that can be turned off, and whether it is on or off by default Enforcement Rules Art. 8(2)(1): planned scope, categories, and specific purpose of processing
2. How long it is kept Retention for inputs, outputs, uploaded files, and logs; how long backups take to clear after deletion Same item: planned period of processing
3. Where it is stored Countries or regions where data is processed and stored, and whether you can choose PDPA Art. 21: international transfers can be restricted in certain cases
4. Which downstream vendors receive it The subprocessor list, and whether you are notified of changes Enforcement Rules Art. 8(2)(3): where there is subcontracting, the agreed subcontractors
5. How you are notified How soon you are told about a breach, what the notice contains, and the remedies Art. 8(2)(4): matters to be notified and remedies for violations
6. What happens at the end How data is exported, when it is deleted, and whether you can get proof of deletion Art. 8(2)(6): return of data media and deletion of stored data when the commission ends

Article 8(3) also requires the commissioning party to regularly confirm how the contractor is performing and to record the results. Article 4 of the Personal Data Protection Act provides that a party commissioned to process personal data is deemed to be the commissioning party within the scope of the Act.

2. Four tips for reading the terms

These are practical steps:

  1. Read the terms for the plan you actually use. With the same vendor, personal accounts, team plans, enterprise contracts, and the API often have different data conditions. Do not infer your plan's conditions from the brand's general description. Personal accounts employees open themselves are outside the company's control; see What are the risks when employees paste company data into ChatGPT?
  2. Screenshot and keep the settings. "Not used for training" is often a switch in the account settings; keep a record of its state at signing.
  3. Record the terms' version and date. Terms change; keep the version in force at signing and compare when it is revised.
  4. Map the data flow. Note which vendor receives questions, files, indexes, logs, and backups. See When AI searches company documents, where does the data go?

3. What international frameworks say

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0: GOVERN 6.1 calls for policies and procedures addressing AI risks from third-party entities; GOVERN 6.2 calls for contingency processes to handle failures or incidents in third-party data or AI systems.
  • NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile): lists "value chain and component integration" as one of 12 generative AI risks, including non-transparent upstream third-party components and improper supplier vetting across the AI lifecycle.

FAQ

Q1: Which data terms should a company check before adopting an AI service?
At least six: data use (including model training), retention period, storage region, downstream vendors (subcontracting), breach notification method and timing, and data return and deletion when the contract ends.
Q2: What does Taiwan's personal data law require when commissioning an AI vendor to process personal data?
Article 8 of the Enforcement Rules of the Personal Data Protection Act requires appropriate oversight of the contractor, covering at least the scope, categories, purpose, and period of processing; the contractor's security measures; subcontracting; notification and remedies for violations; and return and deletion when the commission ends, with regular checks and recorded results.
Q3: Is "we don't train on your data" enough?
No. You also need to know how long data is kept, where it is stored, which downstream vendors receive it, how breaches are notified, and how data is deleted at the end, and confirm that the statement applies to the plan and account settings you actually use.
Q4: What is subcontracting, and why ask about it?
Subcontracting is when the vendor passes data to other companies, such as cloud hosts or outsourced support. Article 8 of the Enforcement Rules lists "the agreed subcontractors, where there is subcontracting" as an oversight item, so get the list of downstream vendors and agree to be notified of changes.
