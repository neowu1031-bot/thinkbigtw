My Child Loves Dinosaurs: From a Picture Book to a Museum

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-31   ·  中文

The short answer

Taiwan has two permanent dinosaur exhibitions accessible to families without travelling abroad. The National Museum of Natural Science in Taichung has a 7-meter animatronic T. rex that moves and roars in the Age of Dinosaurs display; the National Taiwan Museum in Taipei has a 'Mesozoic Rulers' unit in its Ancient Life paleontology hall. For a child who loves dinosaurs, using a book as a starting point—then connecting it to a real museum visit—creates a learning loop that keeps the interest growing.

2. National Museum of Natural Science, Taichung — Life Science Hall

Address: No. 1, Guanqian Road, North District, Taichung City.
The Age of Dinosaurs display on the 1st floor of the Life Science Hall includes:

  • A 7-meter long, 4-meter tall animatronic Tyrannosaurus rex that moves and roars — typically the first thing children run to.
  • A pair of animatronic Velociraptors.
  • Ankylosaur bones.
  • An Oviraptor nest fossil.
  • A Tarbosaurus skull.
  • A juvenile T. rex skeleton.

The 2nd floor covers Archaeopteryx and the definition of dinosaurs (what distinguishes them from other archosaurs). The atrium has flying pterosaurs suspended from the ceiling — often the detail children remember longest.

Note: Taiwan's own fossil sites at Penghu Channel and Zuozhen (Tainan) have Pleistocene land vertebrate fossils ("Penghu fauna" and "Zuozhen fauna") that are significant for understanding prehistoric Taiwan, but these are not dinosaur fossils.

3. National Taiwan Museum Paleontology Hall, Taipei — Ancient Life

Address: 25 Xiangyang Road, Zhongzheng District, Taipei City.
The permanent exhibition "Ancient Life — Epic of Life and Evolution" includes a "Mesozoic Rulers" unit with:

  • A Huanghe Titan sauropod skeleton.
  • A Tarbosaurus skull and partial skeleton.
  • Velociraptor specimens.
  • Pterosaurs, ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs, and mosasaurs.

Per the Ministry of Culture arts platform, the exhibition is running through December 31, 2026.

4. Three ways to connect book and museum visit

Before the visit: Read the book together and find the dinosaurs that appear in the story. Ask "which one do you want to look for first?" This gives the child a goal for the visit.

During the visit: Find the dinosaurs from the book. Take a photo of the child standing in front of the exhibit. Ask one question per exhibit ("how did it move?" or "what did it eat?").

After the visit: Re-read the book and notice what the story got right and what was different from the real fossils. This is the beginning of critical reading—using the real world to evaluate a story.

FAQ

Q1: Where can I take my dinosaur-loving child in Taiwan?
National Museum of Natural Science in Taichung (Life Science Hall 1F: animatronic T. rex, Velociraptors, Ankylosaur, Oviraptor nest, Tarbosaurus skull; atrium: pterosaurs) and National Taiwan Museum in Taipei (Ancient Life hall: Huanghe Titan, Tarbosaurus, Velociraptor, pterosaurs, ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs, mosasaurs).
Q2: Are there real dinosaur fossils from Taiwan?
No. Taiwan's fossil sites at Penghu Channel and Zuozhen (Tainan) have Pleistocene land vertebrate fossils from mammals and other animals—these are significant prehistoric finds, but not dinosaur fossils.
Q3: How do I use a dinosaur picture book with a museum visit?
Before: find the book's dinosaurs and set a museum goal. During: find them in real life and take a photo. After: re-read the book and compare it with what you saw—what was the same, what was different. This builds critical reading alongside the science interest.
