Can You Get a Refund When a Child Buys In-App Items? Costs and Free Features (Taiwan)

Think BIG Q&A Library　·　November 12, 2026　·　中文版

Short answer

Possibly, depending on the case. Under Taiwan's Civil Code, a contract made by a person aged seven to under eighteen without the legal representative's permission takes effect only if the representative approves it, except for matters necessary for daily life given the person's age and status. The 7-day cancellation right in the Consumer Protection Act also has an exception for digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, or online services completed once provided, where the consumer agreed in advance. Actual refunds also depend on store rules and the facts.

1.Contracts made by minors

SituationCivil Code
Under sevenNo capacity (Article 13)
Seven or older, still a minorLimited capacity (Article 13); a declaration of intent needs the legal representative's permission, except for matters that purely benefit the minor or are necessary for daily life given age and status (Article 77)
Contract made without permissionTakes effect only if the legal representative approves it (Article 79)

See the Civil Code in the Laws and Regulations Database (Chinese). Whether a purchase is "necessary for daily life" depends on the amount, purpose and the child's age and status; there is no fixed answer.

2.The 7-day cancellation right and the digital-content exception

A consumer in a distance (communication) transaction can generally cancel within seven days of receiving goods or services without giving a reason (Consumer Protection Act, Article 19). Article 2 of the rules on reasonable exceptions lists exceptions, including digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, or online services completed once provided, supplied only with the consumer's prior consent, where the business has told the consumer that the right is excluded. (Consumer Protection Act (Chinese); exceptions rules (Chinese)) Whether an in-app purchase falls within the exception depends on whether the purchase page gave that notice and obtained consent beforehand.

3.What parents can do before and after paying

1. Check the store page for "includes in-app purchases" and subscription terms. See which features are free, which are paid and whether renewal is automatic.

2. Manage purchases in device settings. Apple's Ask to Buy lets a parent approve a child's purchases and downloads (Apple Support); Google Play lets a family manager require purchase approvals, which cover purchases made through Google Play billing (Google Play Help). Check the actual settings on your own device.

3. If a purchase was a mistake, contact the store and the provider first. Keep the order and payment notices; for disputes you can then approach the consumer protection authority. This page summarises rules; it is not legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Q1: Can I get a refund if my child buys in-app items?
Possibly, depending on the case. Under Article 79 of the Civil Code a contract made by a person with limited capacity without the representative's permission takes effect only if approved, except for matters necessary for daily life (Article 77). Store rules also apply.
Q2: Is there a 7-day cancellation right for digital content?
The Consumer Protection Act gives a 7-day right, but Article 2 of the exceptions rules excludes digital content not on a tangible medium, or online services completed once provided, supplied with the consumer's prior consent and after the business has given notice.
Q3: At what age does a child have limited capacity?
Under Article 13 of the Civil Code, minors aged seven or older have limited capacity; those under seven have no capacity.

The app is in development and has not been released, so this page says nothing about pricing or free features.
