What Age Is Best to Start a Second Language? What the Research Says

Think BIG Q&A Library　·　November 9, 2026　·　中文版

Short answer

There is no single age after which it is too late. One of the largest studies so far (Hartshorne, Tenenbaum and Pinker, 2018, with 669,498 English speakers) estimates that grammar-learning ability stays intact until about age 17.4 and then declines steadily. So the preschool and primary-school years are not late, and an earlier start simply allows more time to build up. The study measured English grammar, so it should not be read as a timetable for Mandarin tones or writing.

1.What the study did

Earlier debates about a critical period for second languages were limited by small samples and by comparing only final attainment. Hartshorne and colleagues collected data from 669,498 native and non-native English speakers and used a model that separates current age, age at first exposure and years of experience, in order to estimate how learning ability itself changes with age. (Cognition, 2018)

ItemFinding
Sample669,498 English speakers, native and non-native
What was measuredEnglish grammar, including both easy and difficult structures
Main resultGrammar-learning ability is preserved until about 17.4 years, then declines steadily
Authors' readingSupports a sharply defined critical period, ending much later than previously thought

2.How to read it without overreaching

It measured English grammar. Mandarin tones, character shapes and handwriting are different skills, and this study did not measure them.

"Ability is preserved" does not mean "learning is guaranteed." The study describes how learning ability changes with age. It does not promise that starting at a given age, or studying for a given time, leads to a certain level. Exposure, chances to use the language and motivation also matter.

Infant hearing is a separate question. In Kuhl and colleagues' 2003 experiment, 9-month-old American infants heard live native Mandarin speakers in 12 laboratory sessions and kept the ability to tell Mandarin speech sounds apart. Infants who heard the same material only in audiovisual or audio recordings showed no such effect. (PNAS, 2003) That experiment measured speech-sound discrimination only, not speaking or reading.

3.What parents can do

No need to rush into a formal course. In the early years, everyday talk, songs and picture books matter most, as long as the child is willing to listen and speak.

Keep a share of real conversation. Apps and videos can supplement, but children still need people to talk with (see Can children learn Mandarin from videos?).

Older than preschool is not too late. By this estimate, learning ability remains until the late teens. Steady use with someone to practice with is easier to control than the starting age.

Frequently asked questions

Q1: What age is best to start a second language?
There is no single best age. Hartshorne and colleagues (2018) analysed 669,498 English speakers and estimated that grammar-learning ability holds until about 17.4 years and then declines, so preschool and primary school are not late; an earlier start allows more accumulated time.
Q2: When does the critical period for a second language end?
The study estimates that grammar-learning ability declines steadily after about 17.4 years, much later than earlier guesses. It measured English grammar and should not be applied directly to Mandarin tones or writing.
Q3: Can babies learn foreign speech sounds from recordings?
In Kuhl and colleagues' 2003 experiment, 9-month-old infants who heard live Mandarin speakers kept their ability to discriminate Mandarin sounds, while infants who heard the same material in recordings did not. It measured speech-sound discrimination only.
Q4: Is it too late if a child missed the toddler years?
No. The research estimates learning ability holds until the late teens, and outcomes also depend on exposure, chances to use the language and motivation.
