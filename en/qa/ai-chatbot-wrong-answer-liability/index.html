Who Is Responsible When an AI Chatbot Gives a Wrong Answer?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-21   ·  中文

The short answer

Responsibility for an incorrect chatbot answer depends on the service, contracts, applicable law, and facts; AI generation alone does not resolve the company’s obligations. Before launch, establish four safeguards: source control, answer boundaries, human escalation, and records with correction procedures. This Taiwan-focused overview is general information, not legal advice.

1. Separate a wrong answer from a legal conclusion

Article 4 of Taiwan’s Consumer Protection Act addresses sufficient and accurate consumer information. Article 22 concerns truthful advertising and related obligations. Whether a particular chatbot statement is advertising or contractual content requires reviewing its presentation and transaction context.

Civil Code Article 184 concerns liability for unlawful infringement of rights through intent or negligence, among other conditions. Whether it applies, or contractual liability arises, needs case-specific review. Retain relevant evidence and define vendor maintenance and notification duties. Internal allocation of work and consumers’ legal rights are separate questions.

2. Build four safeguards into the workflow

These are editorial recommendations, not four steps prescribed by law.

Safeguard Preparation Test
Sources Approved documents, versions, and owners Conflicting old and new policies
Answer scope Permitted topics and prohibited commitments Missing evidence triggers no speculation
Human escalation Working contact and assigned owner Out-of-scope cases reach a person
Records and correction Necessary dialogue, evidence, and change records Reconstruct the version behind an error

NIST AI 600-1 (2024) describes confidently stated false content and fabricated citations. A displayed reference does not establish that every claim is supported. Check claims individually, including omitted exceptions.

3. Contain the error and verify the fix

Suggested response steps are to suspend the affected route or switch it to human handling, preserve necessary evidence, and review affected questions and versions. Have appropriate staff approve corrections or notices. Do not close an issue after a prompt edit without rerunning the original and related cases.

Local deployment can support internal-processing requirements but still needs answer validation. Cloud deployments need data-flow and service-term reviews as well. Define knowledge updates, escalation, incident handling, and acceptance responsibilities in either case.

FAQ

Q1: Does an “AI answers are for reference” notice settle liability?
No. Review the actual interaction, transaction, contract, and applicable law.
Q2: Is the vendor responsible for everything?
Review the contract and actual conduct. Vendor duties do not by themselves determine consumer rights.
Q3: Must a person review every answer?
Set scope by risk; route important commitments, disputes, and unsupported questions to designated staff.
Q4: Do citations prevent wrong answers?
No. NIST notes that citations themselves can be fabricated; open and check them.
