What Should a Company Do When an AI System Has a Security Incident? Five Things to Do First

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-31   ·  中文

The short answer

Do five things first: stop (pause the affected AI feature and revoke leaked keys or permissions), preserve (keep logs and evidence; do not rush to delete), investigate (scope: which data, which people, which time period), report (notify according to your status, industry, and contracts; in Taiwan, government agencies and designated non-government entities must report within one hour of becoming aware, and under the current Article 12 of the Personal Data Protection Act, data subjects must be notified when a violation of the Act causes personal data to be stolen, leaked, altered, or otherwise infringed), and fix (repair the cause and re-verify before going live again). NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 places incident response within the Detect, Respond, and Recover functions and stresses preparation beforehand.

1. What counts as an AI security incident

These situations are specific to, or especially likely in, AI systems:

  • The AI reads a document or web page with hidden instructions and does something it should not (prompt injection). See What is prompt injection?
  • An employee asking the AI sees documents outside their permissions. See How do you make sure each department sees only its own data?
  • The key used to call the AI leaks. See What happens if an AI API key leaks?
  • An AI Agent automatically sends, deletes, or changes production data
  • A third-party package, plugin, or model file has been tampered with

2. How to do the five things

Step What to do Basis
1. Stop Pause the affected AI feature or the AI Agent's automatic actions; revoke leaked keys and tokens; cut outbound connections if needed NIST SP 800-61r3: Respond includes containing the incident
2. Preserve Keep the AI system's logs, conversations, tool-call records, and settings; back up before fixing Enforcement Rules Article 12 lists evidence preservation as a security measure; the 2025 amendment to PDPA Article 12, not yet in force, also requires recording the facts and keeping records
3. Investigate Confirm the scope: which data, which people, since when, and whether it is still happening NIST SP 800-61r3: Detect includes analyzing events
4. Report Notify according to status and contracts: authorities, data subjects, clients, vendors See the next section
5. Fix Repair the cause (permissions, settings, package versions), re-verify with test questions before going live, and write lessons back into the rules NIST SP 800-61r3: Recover and continuous improvement; NIST AI RMF MANAGE 4.3

3. Whom to notify, and how fast

  1. Government agencies and designated non-government entities: under Taiwan's Cyber Security Management Act, designated non-government entities include critical infrastructure providers, state-owned enterprises, certain foundations, and government-controlled entities. Under the Regulations on Reporting, Response and Drills for Cybersecurity Incidents, a government agency must report on the platform designated by the authority within one hour of becoming aware of an incident (Article 6), and under Article 11 a designated non-government entity must report within one hour of becoming aware of an incident, in the manner set by its central competent authority. Article 13 requires damage control or recovery within 72 hours for level 1 or 2 incidents and 36 hours for major incidents, and an investigation, handling, and improvement report within one month.
  2. When personal data is compromised (current text): Article 12 of the Personal Data Protection Act requires a government agency or non-government entity that violates the Act and causes personal data to be stolen, leaked, altered, or otherwise infringed to notify the data subjects in an appropriate manner after investigating. Article 22 of the Enforcement Rules says the notice must include the facts of the infringement and the measures taken.
  3. The 2025 amendment (promulgated, not yet in force): Article 12 as amended on November 11, 2025 adds reporting to the authority when an incident falls within a defined scope, along with immediate and effective response measures and record keeping. The Executive Yuan will set its effective date; as of our check on October 24, 2026, it was not yet in force.
  4. Industry rules and contracts: some industries' authorities have their own rules, and client contracts may set notification deadlines, so check both.

4. Prepare beforehand

NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 notes that the preparation activities of Govern, Identify, and Protect are not part of incident response itself but determine how well an incident can be handled. For AI systems, at least decide in advance: who is authorized to press "pause," where logs are stored, how keys are revoked, and whom to notify. For which logs to keep, see What logs should an AI system keep?

FAQ

Q1: What is the first step when an AI system has a security incident?
Stop first: pause the affected AI feature or the AI Agent's automatic actions, and revoke leaked keys or permissions. Then preserve logs and evidence, investigate the scope, notify as required, and finally fix and re-verify before going live again.
Q2: Must every company in Taiwan report a security incident within one hour?
It depends on the company's status. Under the Regulations on Reporting, Response and Drills for Cybersecurity Incidents, government agencies and designated non-government entities (such as critical infrastructure providers and state-owned enterprises) must report within one hour of becoming aware. Ordinary private companies are not covered by that rule, but under the current Article 12 of the Personal Data Protection Act they must still notify data subjects when a violation of the Act causes personal data to be stolen, leaked, altered, or otherwise infringed, and industry rules or contracts may add requirements.
Q3: Whom must a company notify after a personal data breach in Taiwan?
Under the current Article 12 of the Personal Data Protection Act, it must notify the data subjects in an appropriate manner after investigating, including the facts of the infringement and the measures taken. The 2025 amendment to Article 12 adds reporting to the authority within a defined scope, but the Executive Yuan will set its effective date; as of October 24, 2026, it was not yet in force.
Q4: Can the affected data be deleted right away?
Do not rush to delete. Keep the logs, conversations, tool-call records, and settings first, so the scope and cause can be found; back up before fixing.
