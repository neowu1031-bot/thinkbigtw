How to Start Bedtime Reading: 4 Steps to Build a Lasting Bedtime Routine

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-10-21   ·  中文

The short answer

Bedtime reading can start with 4 steps: set a fixed bedtime, arrange a fixed sequence of quiet activities, place the story after going to bed, and end with a fixed phrase before lights out. Taiwan's National Health Administration recommends building a "bedtime ritual" for children, with an example sequence of toilet, bath, pajamas, teeth, water, bed, story, goodnight, lights out; the American Academy of Pediatrics also notes that all children benefit from a regular bedtime routine, such as "brush, book, bed."

1. What official sources say

Taiwan's Ministry of Health and Welfare National Health Administration's Maternal and Child Care website writes: establishing a "bedtime ritual" for children helps them fall asleep smoothly—that is, performing a series of quiet preparatory activities in a fixed order before sleep to help children relax, with the example sequence of "toilet → bath → pajamas → teeth → water → bed → story → goodnight → lights out." (NHA Maternal and Child Care Website)

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) HealthyChildren website also notes that all children benefit from a regular bedtime routine; for young children, an early start with "brush, book, bed" in that order is recommended. (HealthyChildren.org)

2. Four steps

1. Set a fixed bedtime. The NHA explains that the bedtime is not decided by the child alone, but set by the parent considering the needs of both parent and child, and carried out consistently.

2. Arrange a fixed sequence of quiet activities. With a fixed sequence, the child knows "what comes next." The NHA example above can be used directly and adjusted to fit your family's habits.

3. Place the story after going to bed, before lights out. Read slowly and softly. For book selection, the Ministry of Education Bookstart Reading Handbook notes that children aged 2–3 like to look at the same book repeatedly and are suited to picture books with simple plots, repetitive sentence patterns, and content about everyday experiences such as eating, bathing, brushing teeth, and sleeping. (Bookstart Reading Handbook) So reading the same book every night is fine.

4. End with a fixed phrase. Tell the child in advance that "after this book we say goodnight and turn off the lights," and close the book when it is done. This is a practical recommendation, not from the above sources; the closing phrase is yours to choose—the key is to use the same one every day.

3. Common sticking points

The child keeps asking for one more book. The NHA also mentions that most children like to play and don't want to go to bed, and bedtime struggles are common. Agreeing in advance on "how many books tonight" is easier than refusing in the moment.

Too tired today to read. The routine can be shortened, but try to keep the order: teeth, bed, a short story, goodnight.

FAQ

Q1: How do I start bedtime reading?
Start with 4 steps: set a fixed bedtime, arrange a fixed sequence of quiet activities, place the story after going to bed, and end with a fixed phrase before lights out. The NHA recommends building a "bedtime ritual," with an example sequence including teeth, bed, story, goodnight, and lights out.
Q2: What is a bedtime ritual?
A bedtime ritual is a series of quiet preparatory activities performed in a fixed order before sleep to help children relax. The NHA's example sequence is: toilet, bath, pajamas, teeth, water, bed, story, goodnight, lights out.
Q3: Is it okay to read the same bedtime book every night?
Yes. The Ministry of Education Bookstart Reading Handbook lists "looking at the same book repeatedly" as a characteristic of 2–3-year-olds; this age group is suited to picture books with simple plots and repetitive sentence patterns.
