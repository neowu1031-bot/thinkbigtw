No Tech Background and Official AI Courses Feel Hard to Follow: How Do You Start Learning to Use AI?

Think BIG Q&A Library  ·  2026-11-02   ·  中文

The short answer

You do not have to finish a whole course first. Official courses from AI companies are thorough, and some say beginners are welcome, but the pages are mostly in English and use terms such as prompts, models, and workflows. If you get stuck halfway, change the order: use a free chat AI to finish one small everyday task, then go back to the official course once you have a feel for it. Three habits: learn one thing at a time, use everyday examples, and use free tools. If you want someone to walk you through it step by step, watch Think BIG Academy's "Keeping Up with AI" series (陪你跟上 AI, in Mandarin): BIG explains one topic per episode in 1 to 2 minutes. Start with Episode 1, "I know nothing about AI. Is it too late to start?"

1. What the official courses teach

These are public learning resources from several AI companies (see the official pages for current details):

Course Provider How the official page describes it
Claude Academy Anthropic A learning hub with the line "Learn to use AI safely, effectively, and with intention," including AI Fluency courses
AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations Anthropic Teaches practical skills for effective, efficient, ethical, and safe AI interaction; the page says it suits people new to Claude as well as seasoned AI practitioners
Claude 101 Anthropic Teaches how to use Claude for everyday work tasks and its core features
OpenAI Academy OpenAI Learning tracks such as "Apply AI at Work," "Build with AI," "Lead AI Adoption," and "Teach and learn with AI," plus livestreams and webinars
Google AI Essentials Google States "No experience required"; five modules of videos, readings, and interactive exercises, completed in under 5 hours

These are all good resources. For someone who has never used AI, the usual sticking point is not the course itself but that, without having tried AI, it is hard to picture what the course is describing. Try it yourself a few times first, and many ideas in the course will click.

2. Three habits for complete beginners

  • Learn one thing at a time. Today, learn only how to ask AI to turn your to-do list into three tasks. You do not need models, prompts, and Agents all at once.
  • Use everyday examples. Start with things you do daily: sorting a to-do list, drafting a message to a friend, asking what to cook with what is left in the fridge. The closer to daily life, the easier to remember.
  • Use free tools. Many chat AIs have a free plan; for example, Claude has a Free plan for chatting on the web, desktop, and mobile. Practice on a free plan first, and consider other plans only once you know you will use it.

3. A 5-step beginner path

  1. Pick a free chat AI and sign in. The phone or computer you already have is enough; no new device needed.
  2. In week one, do just one small everyday task. For example, paste in your to-do list and ask it to "turn this into three things I can finish today, and ask me how much time I have first."
  3. State what you want clearly, and keep refining. Say who it is for, the goal, and any limits. If the answer is too long, say "cut it to three lines"; if it is too hard, say "explain it so a middle schooler would understand."
  4. Build a habit of checking. AI can be wrong while sounding confident (see When AI is wrong but sounds confident). Double-check numbers and anything about health, law, or money, and do not paste in ID numbers, passwords, or other people's personal data.
  5. Once you have a feel for it, return to the official courses. AI Fluency, OpenAI Academy, or Google AI Essentials will then connect more easily with your own experience.

FAQ

Q1: Are official AI online courses suitable for complete beginners?
Some say beginners are welcome: Anthropic's AI Fluency page says it suits both newcomers and experienced users, and Google AI Essentials states no experience is required. If you get stuck partway, use a free chat AI for a few everyday tasks first, then go back.
Q2: Do you have to pay to learn AI?
Not necessarily. Many chat AIs have a free plan, such as Claude's Free plan. Demonstrations in Think BIG Academy's "Keeping Up with AI" series also focus on free tools and free plans.
Q3: Can you learn to use AI without English or coding skills?
Yes. For everyday use you can simply type your question in your own language; no coding is needed. Start with one small task, such as sorting a to-do list or drafting a message.
