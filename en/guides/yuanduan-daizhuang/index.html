Remote OpenClaw Installation: Process, Security & How to Choose a Service

Think BIG Editorial

Want someone to install OpenClaw remotely but worried about security? This guide covers only the remote installation itself: what the process looks like, what you'll see during the session, what we explicitly don't do — and five questions to ask any remote installation service before you hand over access.

Updated: 2026-08-31

People searching "someone to install OpenClaw remotely" usually have two concerns: they can't get it installed themselves, and they're not comfortable letting a stranger onto their computer. The first problem is easy — pay someone who knows how. The second concern is the real barrier: you're handing your screen to an engineer you've never met. What gives you reason to trust them?

This guide isn't about whether OpenClaw is worth using (that's covered in the installation overview guide). It covers only the remote installation itself: what the process looks like, what you'll see during the session, what we explicitly don't do — and five questions to use as your checklist for evaluating any service you consider.

What is remote installation? How is it different from following a tutorial?

Remote installation is straightforward: an engineer connects to your computer via remote desktop software and sets up OpenClaw end-to-end, verifies it works, and you watch the whole thing on your screen. You don't touch the terminal, and you don't decode English error messages.

The key difference from following a tutorial yourself is the engineer's judgment. Tutorials can only describe an ideal path, but every computer has different system versions, permission settings, and installed software. When an error appears mid-installation that isn't in the tutorial, that's where experience matters. Much of what you're paying for is that experience with the unexpected.

"Consulting" extends beyond installation: the installation is just the start. What workflows to hand to your AI assistant, what skills you need, how to manage costs — these questions come after installation. Our approach is to understand your needs before quoting a scope, and to quote before charging.

Full process: from inquiry to completion

  1. Brief conversation first: Tell us your computer environment (operating system, rough specs) and what you want the AI assistant to do. If the environment isn't suitable, we'll say so upfront — not after you've placed an order.
  2. Order placed, time scheduled: Contact us and arrange a mutually convenient installation slot. Service hours: Monday to Friday, 10:00–24:00 (UTC+8).
  3. Remote connection established: At the scheduled time, you open AnyDesk or DeskIn and share the session code with the engineer. The connection is established only with your permission — you can disconnect at any time.
  4. Full transparency during installation: Every action the engineer takes happens on your screen. You can see what they open and what they change. When you need to log in to an account or authorize something, you do it yourself — the engineer guides you.
  5. Live sign-off: After installation, we verify together that the AI assistant runs, responds, and that any connected integrations receive messages. The session ends only once this is confirmed.
  6. Session ends immediately after completion: Remote access is disconnected as soon as installation is done. No backdoor software is left on your computer. After that, contacting us is initiated by you — we don't have residual access.

After completion, the Single Agent includes 7-day post-installation technical support, a full personal skill package + 1 custom helper + Coding Agent connection. Ready to use immediately.

Security and trust: what we explicitly don't do

When someone connects remotely to your computer, the real concern isn't "will they install things wrong" — it's "what else did they do?" So rather than tell you to trust us, here's a written list of boundaries you can verify item by item:

  • We never ask for passwords or credentials. Any login needed is performed by you. Passwords and API keys are never transmitted via any chat message.
  • We don't apply for or manage your API keys. When a cloud-based large model is used, the AI model key is applied for by you. The engineer guides you verbally. The key is yours — it goes into your computer's local config file only. We never handle it or retain it. Local version: no API key needed.
  • Keys are not pushed to any cloud version control. Nothing of yours is committed to any cloud repository during the installation.
  • AnyDesk or DeskIn only. We don't accept other remote software, don't use SSH direct connections, and don't do on-site installations. We insist on these two tools because: the connection requires your permission, you can see everything throughout, and sessions are traceable.
  • Official release only. OpenClaw is installed from the official source — not third-party redistributions.
  • Session ends at completion — no backdoors left. Worth repeating.

All of the above is standard practice across all plans — no surcharge. If you need supply-chain scanning or 24-hour monitoring as enterprise-grade security measures, that falls under custom scope and can be discussed separately.

How to evaluate a remote installation service: five checkpoints

This section isn't only for people considering us. The market has multiple services offering OpenClaw installation. Regardless of who you go with, ask them these five questions first:

  1. What tool do you use to connect? The answer should be a named, mainstream remote desktop application (such as AnyDesk or DeskIn) where you grant permission and can disconnect. If they want your SSH credentials or want you to install an unfamiliar connection program, choose someone else.
  2. Will you ever ask for my passwords or API keys? The proper approach is for you to enter them yourself, with the key stored only on your machine. Anyone who asks you to paste credentials into a chat should be immediately disqualified.
  3. Can you still access my computer after installation? The answer should be "no — only if you allow a new session." Ask explicitly whether anything is left behind for persistent remote access.
  4. Have you separated the service fee from AI model usage fees? Model fees are billed per use, paid directly by you — if the service provider doesn't mention this proactively, factor that into how you weigh their other claims.
  5. Is it the official release? Ask "where does the version you're installing come from?" If they can't answer or give a vague answer, the risk is yours to judge.

We operate exactly according to these five criteria, so we're comfortable publishing them as a public checklist. Use them on us or on any other provider.

After installation: what consulting support covers

Installation solves "can't get it set up." Consulting support solves "it's installed but I don't know how to use it effectively." The typical path looks like:

  • Run the standard installed skills for a while to understand what repetitive work you actually do every day.
  • When specific needs emerge, add skill packages — the engineer builds your specific workflows (auto-replies, quotes, scheduled reminders) as custom skills.
  • If you don't want to maintain it yourself, consider an annual plan — the engineer provides ongoing support, updates, and maintenance.

Needs first, specs second. We don't recommend buying to the ceiling from day one. Get the Single Agent working and develop a feel for it; then your money goes exactly where it's needed.

FAQ

Q: I have no programming knowledge. Can someone install OpenClaw for me remotely?

Yes — this is exactly who the remote installation service is for. The engineer installs it remotely via AnyDesk or DeskIn. No technical background is needed. Just turn on your computer, allow the connection, and perform any authorization steps yourself when prompted.

Q: Is letting an engineer connect to my computer remotely safe?

The connection is established only with your permission; you can disconnect at any time; all operations are visible on your screen; the session ends and nothing is retained after installation. We also won't ask for any credentials — any logins required are performed by you. See the "What we explicitly don't do" section above.

Q: How much does remote installation cost? Are there monthly fees?

The Single Agent service fee is NT$7,840, one-time — no ongoing subscription from us. Local version: no model usage fees. Cloud version: AI model API usage is billed per use by the model provider — separate from the service fee.

Q: Can you do on-site installation or SSH direct connection?

Neither. We only do remote installation via AnyDesk or DeskIn — no other methods, no on-site. The restriction is intentional: it keeps the entire process visible and traceable.

Q: Can you apply for my API key on my behalf?

No — we don't apply on your behalf or manage your keys. You apply for the API key yourself; the engineer walks you through the process verbally. The key goes into your local config file only — never transmitted via chat and never pushed to any cloud version control.

Q: What do I need to prepare for the installation session?

A computer that meets minimum specs and is connected to the internet, with AnyDesk or DeskIn installed, plus any service accounts you need to connect (you'll log in yourself when needed). The pre-session conversation will confirm your environment — no surprises on the day.

Q: What if something goes wrong after installation? Can you still access my computer?

No — by design. The session ends at installation completion. The Single Agent includes 7-day post-installation technical support; if a new remote session is needed, you allow the connection again at that time.

Q: What if the installation can't be completed? Is there a refund?

If the installation cannot be completed due to an issue on our side, we provide a full refund in accordance with applicable consumer protection regulations.

Conclusion: build trust on what you can verify

Hiring someone to install OpenClaw remotely is ultimately a trust transaction. Our answer isn't "trust us" — it's making the process and boundaries into a list you can verify item by item: you grant the connection, you see the operations, you enter your own credentials, the session ends at completion, the fee structure is stated in two parts.

You can use this standard to evaluate us — or to evaluate anyone else. Only a service that passes this check is worth handing your screen to.
